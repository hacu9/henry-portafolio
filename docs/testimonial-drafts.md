# Draft testimonials to send for approval

Writing the draft for someone is normal and it is why most LinkedIn
recommendations exist at all. People want to help and do not want to write.
You send a draft, they edit or approve it, they post it under their own name.
At that point it is theirs, it is true, and it survives being clicked.

What you cannot do is publish these as-is. Until a real person has agreed to
one, it is not a testimonial, it is a sentence I wrote.

Each draft below is tied to something specific and verifiable, because a
recommendation that could describe anyone is worth nothing.

---

## 1. For a Level60 colleague who reviewed your pull requests

This is the most valuable one on the list. It is third-party evidence of
technical direction, which is the thing the site is thinnest on.

> Henry set the branch rules our team still works to. Before that we merged
> straight to main and found out later; after it, nothing landed without a
> review. He did not ask permission, he wrote it up, explained why, and made it
> easy to follow. He is the person on the team who notices the process problem
> nobody else has framed yet.

**Ask:** whoever has approved your PRs at Level60.

---

## 2. For whoever worked with you on the Microsoft integrations

> When Microsoft retired the TeamworkDevice endpoint, Henry worked out exactly
> what the replacement could and could not do before anyone touched the
> integration. That turned what could have been weeks of discovery in
> production into a known piece of work. He does the reading first.

**Ask:** the colleague or lead closest to that migration.

---

## 3. For a manager or client who saw you deliver end to end

> Most engineers I have worked with own their part. Henry owns the outcome. He
> built it, deployed it, kept it running, and when the numbers were not right
> he went and found out why instead of waiting to be told. You do not have to
> project-manage him.

**Ask:** a Level60 manager, or the clinic marketplace client.

---

## 4. For anyone who has seen your AI workflow

> Henry uses AI more heavily than anyone I work with, and trusts it less than
> anyone I work with. He runs a second model against his own work specifically
> to catch what the first one got wrong. He has corrected confident, wrong
> answers more than once, including in his own favour.

**Ask:** anyone who has paired with you or seen your review process.

---

## How to send one

> Hola [name], I am putting a portfolio together and I would rather have two
> honest recommendations than ten generic ones.
>
> I drafted something below so you do not have to start from a blank page.
> Change anything, cut anything, or write something completely different. It
> only helps if it is what you actually think.
>
> [paste the draft]
>
> And if you would rather not, that is genuinely fine. Just say so.

## Then

Paste the approved text into `src/data/testimonials.ts` with their real name,
their role at the time, and a link to their profile. The section appears
automatically in all variants and on the CV.
